The one definition and the one formula — say these cold

Definition. "Available-for-sale is the one number a channel may still promise for a SKU at a node. It is not S/4's stock figure. S/4 owns the physical book — unrestricted stock per SKU, plant and storage location, which we called Available. ISM takes that book, applies the lower-of-two rule against the warehouse floor, subtracts everything committed or sold but not yet posted, and publishes AFS per SKU, node and channel. The order system reads it and never computes its own."

Formula. "Basis is the lower of S/4 Available and the WMS on-hand; AFS equals Basis minus Picking Reserve minus Ecom Pick minus Ecom Pack minus Shipped-Not-Billed. Merchandise Reserve and Lost-and-Found sit outside Available by construction, so they are never subtracted twice. On the worked SKU: Basis is the lower of 1,000 and 980, so 980; buckets 200 plus 120 plus 60 plus 40 are 420; AFS is 560."

Financial On-Hand (the honest sentence). "Financial On-Hand is Finance's number — Available plus Merchandise Reserve plus Lost-and-Found — with Shipped-Not-Billed carried as its own reconciling column so Finance sees what has left the floor but not yet posted; it ties to MB5L at month-end at tolerance zero." FOH expansion: canon says Financial On-Hand; "free on hand" is not in any source — see Verify #1

Situation / Task — the business process problem, scope, scale, baselines

S — Two books, one word. MCS and S/4 each published an "available"; Finance, Merchandising, dotcom, the stores and the DC each defined it differently; the stores' sales reached S/4 only as a nightly aggregate, the OMS committed units S/4 could not see, and the WMS floor disagreed with the book by whatever had moved since the last snapshot. Oversell was recurring — no count in the record — and reconciliation was argument.

T — Write the one definition of available that every org would sign; assign one system of record per bucket; make one engine publish it per SKU × node × channel; and prove daily, at tolerance zero, that the engine's copy of the book equals the book.

Baselines on the table: 2 books · "available" defined per org · oversell recurring (record, no count) · reconciliation by hand (≈4 FTE, benchmark) · 60+ stakeholders, about a dozen impacted systems (record) · a 30M-order illustrative base for the model.

Facet 5 — probe questions (challenge Q&A tuned to this interviewer)

Infra — where is the single source of truth for "available," and why isn't it S/4?

Physical stock truth is S/4, full stop. Promisable truth is ISM's published AFS, because S/4 posts documents and settles store sales as a nightly aggregate while promising needs every click and scan. The two are reconciled, not merged: ISM's copy of Available must equal MB52 daily at tolerance zero, and Financial On-Hand must tie to MB5L at month-end.

Infra — is Financial On-Hand before or after reserves?

It includes them. FOH = Available + Merchandise Reserve + Lost-and-Found — it is Finance's physical number, so a held unit is still on hand. AFS is the number after reserves and after every committed bucket. Reserve sits outside Available by construction, so it is never subtracted from AFS twice. Shipped-Not-Billed is carried as its own column so Finance can see what has left the floor but not yet posted. see Verify #1–2 on the SNB term

Infra — what is the join key between S/4 and ISM, and where does it drift?

MATNR × WERKS × LGORT maps one-to-one to an ISM node through the node map; the sold-not-posted bucket joins on Aggregation# back to POS lines. It drifts in two places: an unmapped movement type — a new BWART nobody told ISM about, quarantined, never guessed — and a late goods receipt that crosses the snapshot watermark, which is why the drift report attributes timing rows before anyone calls a Sev.

Infra — who owns the publish?

ISM's AFS engine is the only publisher — per SKU × node × channel, key MATNR × node × channel × version, event to republish p95 under 60 seconds. The OMS, dotcom and the stores read it and never compute. At cutover no SKU could be published from two locations at any instant: zero on the old publish, then 100% on the new.

Infra — why not just trust MARD?

On S/4 MARD is trustworthy — it is a view over MATDOC, not a drifting aggregate — but it answers "what is booked," not "what may be promised." It cannot see a web order placed at 09:14 or a parcel that left at 15:30 before the aggregate posts. So I used it as the anchor of the identity and built the reports to prove every other term against it.

Definitional — why isn't SNB in on-hand? Or is it?

It is inside Finance's book and outside the promise. Until the posting, S/4 Available still holds the 12 sold units, so any on-hand built from Available carries them; SNB is its own column — 60 and 12 — so Finance sees what tonight's posting removes. AFS subtracts it: 60 − 12 = 48. After the match both fall together: 48 and 0. The two canon phrasings of FOH differ on whether SNB is "added"; the arithmetic that cannot be wrong is that the sold unit is counted once.

Say: "SNB is the reconciling column between Finance's book and the promise — in the row Finance reads, out of the number dotcom reads, and never counted twice." Verify #2

Definitional — what if S/4 and ISM disagree at T+1 on a store?

At a store the only sold-not-posted bucket is SNB, so a T+1 difference is a posting that did not match or a line that did not arrive. Example: S/4 posted 12, ISM matched 10 → SNB 2, ISM Available 48 = MB52 48 (Test A passes) but the aging report shows the 2-unit line at 24 hours — that is the control, not the drift check. If Test A itself breaks, it is a posting after the watermark or a duplicate — timing or defect, re-cut or replay, never an edit to the book.

Say: "At a store, T+1 disagreement lives on the aging report, and it is a Sev at 48 hours with a cause, never a plug."

ISM publishes FOH because it holds the sold-not-posted units S/4 will only see at the aggregate; S/4 owns the posted book, the settlement authority and cost.
