    Why: my sensor was a ticket. I need his sensor. A good answer names the break that costs the most and the clock on which someone owns it.

C. The metrics, and which one he is behind on

  1. Of cost per package, on-time delivery, speed, and config error rate, which one is this team most behind on right now, and which of the others are you willing to trade this quarter to move it?

    Why: I will not optimize a metric he is not behind on. A good answer is one metric, and the trade he will actually sign.

  2. How do you decompose cost to serve per package, and which term is a config choice rather than a rate you cannot touch?

    Why: sixty-six cents was useless until it split into tickets and platform. A good answer is a split, and one term an L5 can move by changing a config.

D. The first ninety days

  1. What does an L5 on this team own in the first ninety days: a metric, a set of lanes, a config domain? What does day ninety look like that is not a tour of the network?

    Why: I want a countable object. A good answer is one domain, one metric, and one review I am expected to run.

E. Automation and models

  1. Where is a model already choosing a route, a pull time, or a sort plan, and where is a person still the config because you already know how the model is wrong?

    Why: he has said he wants the limitation of a product early. That is the human loop I kept until a source held its rate. A good answer names the limitation and the re-entry rule.

  2. When a model recommendation and a planner disagree, who wins, and what evidence reopens the decision?

    Why: my release rule was two-sided and signed. The formula did not silently re-plan a lane. A good answer is a written rule, not "the model is usually right."

F. How success is judged

  1. How is this team judged at OP1 and OP2: the metric, the narrative, and the miss that still gets an L5 a hard question in that room?

    Why: I have fed a leadership readout from a weekly review, and the grain of that room is the grain I will run to. A good answer is the one number that shows up, and an example of a miss that was well received because it was early.

The question to end on

  1. If I were here in six months, what would you want me to have made boring, a config change, a cost line, or an exception, that is not boring today?

    Why: it asks for the real pain in his words, and it gives you something to close on. A good answer is one concrete object, not a list of values.
