Here is how I understand a capital-equipment program on a system of record. Correct me where Apple does it differently.

It starts with a program need and a capacity model: units per hour, yield, shifts, lines. That becomes a list of tools and fixtures, then an appropriation with a cost center and a program code. 1. Lifecycle of capital equipment at a contract manufacturer

Program need → capacity model (units per hour, yield, shifts, lines) → equipment list, the bill of tools and fixtures → budget and approval (capital appropriation, cost center, program) → sourcing (GSM: RFQ, quote, purchase order) → supplier build → FAT then SAT → ship and install at the CM → asset tagging and capitalization (asset master, placed-in-service date, depreciation start, location, custodian) → ramp → sustaining, including retrofit and reuse across programs → disposition (transfer, idle, or scrap).

Swimlane. Six stages, four lanes. Read left to right. The books lane does not invent the physical fact; it records the fact the site and the GSM already own.

G. Product improvement idea — ask it, do not pitch it

The question, about 45 seconds, only if invited

The profile says the system of record is the foundation for later AI. I would not put a model on a field I cannot reconcile, because a wrong join compounds. The thing I would want in the first pilot is a weekly data-integrity scorecard, per program and per input owner: completeness and validity on the fields that actually move the book, exception aging, purchase-order to asset reconciliation with the denominator held still, and a flag on any field that only one person can explain. Onboarding playbooks would be measured on a first-time-right rate, not on a go-live date. Does that grain already exist, and is the gap the owner — or is the gap the view?

Three metrics, defined before the first file arrives.

  • Reconciliation. Tagged assets divided by purchase-order lines in scope. In-transit stays in the denominator until the row closes or scope is cut.
  • First-time-right. Share of the ten high-risk fields that pass validation on first load, by program and by owner.
  • Exception aging. Share of open exceptions older than the age bar Finance and the Data Integrity PM set in the pilot. I will not invent that bar in the room.

MVP in six weeks, one pilot program. Weeks 1–2: field dictionary and a named owner for each of the ten fields. Weeks 3–4: one reconciliation extract, PO to invoice to asset, and an exception queue with owner and age. Weeks 5–6: one weekly view the forum actually reads, even if the refresh is a query. A polished visual can wait.

What I would not build first. An anomaly model, a contract-manufacturer portal, a backfill of every historical program, or a leaderboard. The second, smaller idea is the pre-migration harness: schema, duplicate and serial checks, referential checks, and a counted-rows go or no-go before any Excel file touches the system — the same shape as Negative AFS clean at T+1, and as the issues-log triage that separated go-live from fast-follow. That harness is the entry bar to the scorecard, not a second product.

One risk. If the view shames owners, they go back to the side file. I would design it as that owner’s queue, reviewed in their forum, with Data Integrity accountable for the queue and the business owner accountable for the value.
