Story: Sephora Utah DC dual inventory, discovery → architecture council → cutover → hypercare, March 2024 to October 2025. record and record-from-docs. If Q2 has already been told, open with "same umbrella program, different seam."

Situation/Task + Action + Results — 220 words

SITUATION/TASK

In spring 2024 Sephora's Utah distribution center ran as two systemic warehouses inside one building, dotcom and retail, transferring stock to itself at duplicate handling cost. Leadership wanted one omni pool, with a warehouse-system upgrade and robotics ramp on the same path, an October-to-December peak freeze, and zero customer-facing incidents allowed. I owned the program end to end: thirty build tracks, sixty-plus stakeholders, about a dozen core systems I did not own. The targets were zero incidents and processing efficiency against the pre-conversion baseline, measured by DC Operations.

ACTION

Discovery ran from the March 29 kickoff to April 30, each system owner estimating their own row, 44 requirement rows across 23 systems. I took the design through the architecture council as three decisions plus a written fence: challenge May 15, sign-off July 31, specification August 14. I staged two go-lives so each carried one defect class, purchasing and inbound in January 2025, the inventory flip in May. One issues log was the go/no-go: blocker and critical open at zero, not percent complete. In hypercare I read the burn-down daily.

RESULTS

Both go-lives landed with zero customer-facing incidents. DC Operations measured processing efficiency up 15 percent. The log carried 836 rows, 714 closed, 85.4 percent; after the May go-live it spiked to 271 rows in June and burned down 65, 21, 14, 4.

IF THEY PROBE

What slipped? The functional specification: I printed July 26 on the council slide and it was signed August 14. Robotics retail testing slipped three weeks and was re-slotted in the open. Both go-lives held; I never say the chain held to the day.

What did the robotics gate do? Published at 98 percent pick-accurate throughput before a bot took volume. Week two read 94; I moved about 30 percent of volume to carts while the zone logic was fixed; full automation in week three.

Were 836 issues a fire? No. Only about one in seven was a bug; the rest were requirements, action items, questions and parked decisions. The log was the work-triage system.

What was the data-integrity input? Which location holds the inventory and which reserve may promise it. I refused a historical conversion and kept three reserve buckets that already worked.

Who owned the floor? DC Operations ran the floor and measured the 15 percent. The platform and robotics teams configured. Mine were the model, the staging, the gates, the council pack and the go/no-go.

Q3. "How do you identify, investigate and resolve data discrepancies across systems or teams?"

Story: the S/4HANA and ISM daily drift and Negative AFS procedure, step by step. record on the mechanism; FTE figures are model. Alternate: Austria routing guide, in the probe block.

Situation/Task + Action + Results — 214 words

SITUATION/TASK

On the Sephora inventory integration two books had to agree every day: the S/4 stock list and the ISM ledger that publishes available-for-sale. Before, reconciliation was by hand, about four people's effort, and mismatches were argued rather than root-caused. My task was a reconciliation with a tolerance of zero, a named owner, and a path that fixed the source rather than the report.

ACTION

Six steps. One, the daily drift report compares ISM to the S/4 stock list per SKU and node, tolerance zero. Two, timing rows are attributed first: a late goods receipt across the snapshot cut is timing, not drift. Three, the remainder is classified by cause: an unmapped movement type is quarantined, never guessed; a replayed event is caught by its idempotency key. Four, the Negative AFS report must be clean at T plus one; a surviving negative goes to Inventory Control as a row, not a summary. Five, the fix goes into the source, and the next day's report proves it. Six, every correction goes through the AFS Correction screen with a role and an audit row.

RESULTS

Drift went from unmeasured to zero SKUs on consecutive days at hypercare exit. Reconciliation went from about four people to about one, by my model. Oversell went from recurring to zero through both go-lives.

IF THEY PROBE

Give me one where the explanation in the room was wrong. At Elementum's Austria site the report said data quality was 40 percent and everyone blamed carriers. I pulled three years of shipments in SQL: it was about 55, and the defects were ours, missing rates, wrong-grain lanes, five spellings of one carrier. Worked down weekly by defect class, it reached about 99 percent within a quarter, and hand reconciliation fell from eight to ten hours a week to one to two.

What do you do with a row you cannot explain? It stays open with an owner and an age. I do not type a guess to turn a tile green.

Across systems with no common key? Count events in versus events out at every boundary and give the record a trace identifier. At Flexport a missing trace identifier cost two to four hours per bug just to find the row; with it, about an hour.

How do you know the number is fixed? The same report that found it goes green the next day, and stays green two days running.
