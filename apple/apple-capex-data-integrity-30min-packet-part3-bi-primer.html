How a BI tool works, using Looker as the example

Call. CapEx Systems Onboarding Data Integrity Program Manager, with the Capital Expenditure Systems Lead. Thirty minutes. Tableau is a nice-to-have; Power BI or Tableau is preferred. Explain the tool. Do not pretend to be the BI engineer.
How to use it. Section 1 is the model. Section 2 is Looker, only as deep as a follow-up. Section 6 is what you say, including the honest proficiency line.

1. The seven layers of any BI tool

Looker, Tableau, Power BI, and a careful spreadsheet are the same stack. The chart is layer 5. Integrity is decided in the semantic layer and in governance, and it is lost in the connection when nobody knows how fresh the copy is.

LayerWhat it isWhere integrity breaks
1 · SourcesThe warehouse, the ERP, the system of record, and the spreadsheet a team still keeps.A tile with no source system cannot be reconciled to a PO or an asset.
2 · ConnectionLive / direct query asks the warehouse each time: fresh, as fast as the source, and the number is the source’s number. Import / extract copies rows and refreshes on a schedule: fast, and only as fresh as the last refresh.Trust follows the refresh. A missed refresh is a silent old truth. People believe the tile, not the timestamp.
3 · Semantic layerOne definition per metric. Joins and grain live here. A dimension is what you group or filter by (site, program, owner). A measure is the aggregation (count, sum, a ratio).Two formulas with the same name. This is the layer a data-integrity program exists to own.
4 · Query engineTurns the picked fields into SQL, checks a result cache, and may answer from a pre-aggregate instead of the raw rows.A cache with no invalidation trigger still serves yesterday after the load has finished.
5 · VisualizationTiles, filters, drill-down from the number to the rows that made it.A filter default can hide the exceptions the scorecard is for.
6 · DistributionThe dashboard, a scheduled delivery, an alert when a tile crosses a threshold, an embed in the tool people already open.Two emailed files diverge the moment someone edits a formula in one of them.
7 · GovernanceRow-level security, roles, version control, lineage from the tile back to the column, and a certified mark on the content people should trust.Without lineage you cannot answer why a field was filled in. That question is the job.
Excel is weak at the semantic layer. A formula in a cell binds nobody. Copy the workbook and the definition forks — five files, five meanings of reconciled — with no row-level security that survives a forward, no lineage back to the source column, and no certified version. Excel can hold rows during a migration. It cannot stop the next copy from changing the rule.

2. Looker specifically

What you are allowed to claim. You can explain this. You have not shipped LookML. Section 6 has the sentence. Looker here means Looker on Google Cloud, the product with LookML. Looker Studio (formerly Data Studio) is a different, lighter product: reports on top of connectors, no LookML, no Git-backed semantic model. If they say “Looker,” ask which one in the first sentence, then talk about this one.

Looker does not store the business data. At query time it generates SQL and runs it on the warehouse you point it at — BigQuery, Snowflake, Redshift, and the usual others. What Looker itself keeps is the LookML, the saved content, users and permissions, and a result cache. A persistent derived table is still a table in the warehouse; Looker builds and refreshes it there. The book of record never moves into Looker.

LookML objectWhat it is, in the order you meet it
ProjectA Git repository of LookML. This is the unit you branch, review, and merge.
ModelWhich warehouse connection, which explores are exposed, and who may open them.
ViewOne table, or one derived table, plus its dimensions and measures. The definition of a column and of a metric lives here.
ExploreA starting view plus its joins. This is the self-serve surface: the field picker a GSM or a finance partner actually uses. They never see the SQL unless someone opens the SQL tab.

What the model contains

PieceThe sentence you need
DimensionAn attribute. Site, program, supplier, a status. You group by it and filter on it. You do not sum it.
MeasureAn aggregate: count, sum, count_distinct, or a ratio written sql: ${a} / NULLIF(${b}, 0). count_distinct on the primary key is the fan-out-safe habit. A raw count counts joined rows.
Dimension groupOne timestamp becomes a date, a week, and a month (type: time, with timeframes). Aging buckets are built on that date, not retyped in every tile.
JoinDeclared on the explore, with a cardinality: one-to-one, many-to-one, one-to-many, many-to-many. Looker can rewrite a sum so a one-to-many join does not double-count, but only if the cardinality is declared correctly. A ratio you write by hand is not protected for you. Wrong cardinality, wrong total.
Derived tableA view defined by SQL you write, or by a native derived table (Looker builds the SQL from an explore). Use it when the grain you need is not a raw table — one row per line per field, for completeness.
PDTA persistent derived table: the same idea, materialized in the warehouse and rebuilt on a trigger (a datagroup, often “rebuild when max(loaded_at) changes”). It is a pre-aggregate for speed. It is still warehouse data. A PDT that does not rebuild is a stale extract with a better name.
Aggregate awarenessYou declare rollup tables. Looker uses the smallest rollup that can answer the question, and falls back to the base table when you ask for a field the rollup does not have. That is how a weekly total stays fast and a drill to the PO line still works.
CachingIdentical SQL can be served from the result cache. Tie the cache to the same datagroup as the load. No trigger means a Monday tile can contradict a query run a minute later.

What a person touches, and what makes the definition auditable

An Explore is the self-serve surface. A Look is one saved query. A dashboard is a layout of tiles; each tile is a query, not a picture. Filters narrow a tile. drill_fields are the columns you land on when you click a number. A link opens the same record in the system of record — the PO, the asset — so the tile is not a dead end. A scheduled delivery sends the dashboard on a clock (Monday 07:00). An alert fires when a tile crosses a threshold, which a schedule does not do. A Looker Action sends rows onward (a ticket, a webhook) when a person or a schedule asks it to.

Row-level security is a user attribute plus an access_filter. The attribute (the program ids this GSM or this CM may see) is set on the user. The filter is injected into the SQL and the user cannot remove it. Content sits in folders; roles (viewer, explorer, developer, admin, or a custom role) decide who may look, explore, or edit LookML. None of that is a substitute for warehouse permissions. It is the application of them.

LookML in Git is the audit trail. A developer branches, Looker validates the LookML, a pull request is reviewed, and production is the merge. The commit is who changed the definition of reconciled, when, and from what. A Look saved in a personal folder is not that. The metric people steer by has to be the merged one.

view: po_lines {
  sql_table_name: capex.po_lines ;;
  dimension: po_line_id {
    primary_key: yes
    type: string
    sql: ${TABLE}.po_line_id ;;
  }
  dimension: site_id {
    type: string
    sql: ${TABLE}.site_id ;;
    description: "Receiving site. Blank means the input owner has not set it."
  }
  dimension: received_date {
    type: date
    sql: ${TABLE}.received_date ;;
  }
  measure: po_line_count {
    type: count_distinct
    sql: ${po_line_id} ;;
    description: "Lines received. Distinct so an asset join cannot double-count."
  }
  measure: null_site_count {
    type: count_distinct
    sql: ${po_line_id} ;;
    filters: [site_id: "NULL"]
    description: "Lines with no site. A completeness exception, not a hidden filter."
  }
  measure: reconciliation_rate {
    type: number
    sql: 1.0 * ${assets.tagged_asset_count} / NULLIF(${po_line_count}, 0) ;;
    value_format_name: percent_1
    description: "Assets tagged / PO lines received. One definition of reconciled."
  }
}

view: assets {
  sql_table_name: capex.assets ;;
  dimension: asset_id { primary_key: yes  type: string  sql: ${TABLE}.asset_id ;; }
  dimension: po_line_id { type: string  sql: ${TABLE}.po_line_id ;; }
  measure: tagged_asset_count {
    type: count_distinct
    sql: ${asset_id} ;;
  }
}

explore: po_lines {
  join: assets {
    type: left_outer
    relationship: one_to_many
    sql_on: ${po_lines.po_line_id} = ${assets.po_line_id} ;;
  }
}

Teaching snippet, not a model you have run. Three dimensions on po_lines (po_line_id, site_id, received_date), three measures (po_line_count, null_site_count, reconciliation_rate), one join. The assets view exists only so the ratio has a real numerator. Cardinality is one PO line to many assets; both counts are count_distinct.

3. How one query flows, end to end

  1. The user opens the Explore and picks fields — program, site, reconciliation rate — and a filter.
  2. Looker reads the LookML and builds the SQL: the columns, the join, the aggregates, and any access filter.
  3. It checks the result cache. A hit returns the stored rows and does not touch the warehouse.
  4. A miss runs the SQL on the warehouse (or on a PDT, which is still a warehouse table).
  5. The warehouse returns the aggregated rows.
  6. Looker renders the tile.
  7. The user drills. Drill fields return the row-level detail behind the number, still under the same access filter.
  8. A link opens that record in the system of record — the PO, the asset — where the input owner can fix the field.
Where it goes wrongWhat the integrity program does about it
Stale extractA Friday refresh, read on Monday, misses a weekend receipt. The rate looks finished. Require a visible “loaded at,” and treat a cache with no datagroup trigger as the same defect.
Two measures, one nameBoth called reconciliation rate. One divides tagged assets by lines received; the other divides by lines ordered. The semantic layer allows one of them in production.
A join that fans outOne line, two assets. A plain count counts the line twice, the denominator inflates, and the rate looks healthier than the book. Declare one-to-many and count distinct on the line id.
A filter default nobody seesThe explore opens on status = Received. Open and in-transit lines never appear, so the scorecard is green because the exceptions were filtered out. Defaults are visible, or they are a bug.
A field with no descriptionPlaced-in-service date with a blank description. Nobody knows whether it means acceptance or the day depreciation starts. The description is the “why” behind the input, written where the field is picked.

5. Worked example — the CapEx data-integrity scorecard in Looker

Illustrative, end to end. This is the scorecard from the product idea, drawn as a Looker explore so you can walk it. It is not Apple’s tool, and none of the counts below are a program you ran. Every figure on this page is ILLUSTRATIVE.

Tables behind the explore: programs, purchase_orders, po_lines, assets, suppliers, sites, input_owners. The explore starts at po_lines, one row per received line. It joins assets one-to-many (a line can tag more than one asset — the fan-out), programs and sites many-to-one, and input_owners many-to-one on the line’s owner. purchase_orders and suppliers are many-to-one parents, joined only when a tile needs the PO number or the supplier name. They are not on the fan-out path. Reconciliation measures are count_distinct on the line id and on the asset id. A plain count would count a line once per asset and make the rate look healthier than the book.

Field completeness is a different grain: one row per line per field (placed-in-service date, site, cost center, program allocation, reuse source asset), because each field has its own owner. That derived table is one-to-many off the line. Its measures count distinct lines inside one field. Joining it into the reconciliation tile would multiply the denominator. Two tiles, two grains, one explore only if the joins are the safe cardinality.

Each GSM and each CM gets a user attribute, allowed_program_ids. The access filter is in the SQL, and they cannot clear it. A scheduled delivery goes out Monday at 07:00 to the input owners on the completeness tile — the people who can fill the null, not a general list. An alert, separate from the schedule, fires when reconciliation falls below the team’s threshold. Ninety-eight percent in the sketch is illustrative; you do not invent the real line. The description on placed-in-service date says whether the field means acceptance or the day depreciation starts. That is the why behind the input, sitting where the field is picked.

What changes in behavior. A null site is the contract manufacturer’s row, not a shared inbox. An exception has an age, and the one past thirty-one days is a close-out conversation with an owner. “Reconciled” is the merged measure. A side spreadsheet that divides tagged assets by lines ordered is a different metric, and it cannot replace the tile without a pull request.

6. How to say it in the call

Sixty seconds — how a BI tool works

A BI tool is seven layers, and the chart is only the fifth. The rows stay in the warehouse, the ERP, or the system of record. The connection is either live, meaning the tool asks the source every time, or an extract, a copy that is only as fresh as its last refresh. The layer that protects data integrity is the semantic layer: one name, one formula, the join and the grain written down, so reconciled means one thing on every tile. The query engine turns that into SQL, checks a cache, and sometimes uses a pre-built aggregate. People see a tile, filter it, and drill to the rows. Distribution is the dashboard, the Monday email, and an alert. Governance decides who sees which programs, and whether that definition is versioned. Excel can hold the rows. It does not hold a definition. The next copy of the file is a second meaning of reconciled.

Ten terms, if they use one

TermOne sentence
Semantic layerThe place a metric is defined once — name, SQL, grain, join — so every tile uses that definition.
DimensionSomething you group or filter by: site, program, owner, a date. You do not sum it.
MeasureAn aggregate: a count, a sum, a count distinct, or a ratio of two of those.
ExploreThe self-serve surface in Looker: one starting view, its joins, and the field picker.
LookMLLooker’s modeling language. It lives in Git. Production is a merge, which is what makes a definition auditable.
PDTA persistent derived table: a pre-aggregate Looker builds and refreshes in the warehouse, not inside Looker.
Fan-outA one-to-many join repeats the left-hand row. A plain count then double-counts. Count distinct on the key, or declare the cardinality and mean it.
Row-level securityA filter the tool applies for you, from who you are. In Looker that is a user attribute plus an access filter. The user cannot take it off.
Extract vs liveAn extract is a scheduled copy, fast and possibly stale. Live, or direct query, asks the source at query time and is only as fast as that source.
Certified contentThe dataset or dashboard a steward has marked as the one to trust. In Tableau that is a badge on a published source; in Power BI, an endorsement. In Looker the equivalent is LookML merged to production, not a Look in a personal folder. Looker Studio is a different product — do not invent a Looker badge.
